Stage 7 · Do the amino acids track sleep?

Weeks 7–8about 5 hours

The central question of the project. GRM1 458 and SIK3 783 differ between mammals. Do the mammals with another amino acid than the human one sleep a different amount? A simple test says yes – and a test that knows the family tree changes the answer.

You will learn

  • to compare two groups: a plot, the means, a t-test
  • why species are not independent data – and phylogenetic regression with phylolm
  • to read a null result: what it does and does not show

1. Join the amino acids to the sleep times

Open the notebook of this stage, read the two tables, and keep GRM1 458. merge joins the rows whose genome_species (in the sleep table) is the same as species (in yours):

sleep <- read.delim("/data/examples/fnss/sleep/mammal_sleep.tsv")
res <- read.delim("~/project/results/species_residues.tsv")
grm <- res[res$variant == "GRM1_S458A", c("species", "residue")]
d <- merge(sleep, grm, by.x = "genome_species", by.y = "species")
nrow(d)
table(d$residue)
d1 <- d[!is.na(d$sleep_pos_h), ]
table(d1$residue)
d1$genome_species[duplicated(d1$genome_species)]

Some genome species stand for more than one sleep species – the olive baboon's sequence for four kinds of baboon, the crab-eating macaque's for three other macaques. They have the same amino acid, so they are not separate evidence about it: average them, so that each genome species counts once. aggregate computes the mean of sleep_pos_h for each combination of the columns after the ~:

per <- aggregate(sleep_pos_h ~ genome_species + residue, data = d1, FUN = mean)
nrow(per)
table(per$residue)
Check

76 sleep species have a GRM1 amino acid (49 S, 27 T); 51 of them have a Phylogeny of Sleep value (40 S, 11 T). After averaging: 46 genome species, 35 S and 11 T.

2. A first test

tapply(per$sleep_pos_h, per$residue, mean)
stripchart(sleep_pos_h ~ residue, data = per, vertical = TRUE, method = "jitter", pch = 19,
           xlab = "Amino acid at GRM1 458", ylab = "Hours of sleep (Phylogeny of Sleep)")
t.test(sleep_pos_h ~ residue, data = per, var.equal = TRUE)
per[per$residue == "T", ]

A t-test asks whether the difference between the means of two groups is larger than would be expected by chance if there were no real difference; the P-value is how often a difference at least this large would turn up by chance alone. stripchart shows every species as a dot; method = "jitter" moves the dots a little sideways so they do not hide each other.

Check

The S species sleep 11.5 hours on average, the T species 8.2: 3.3 hours less, and P = 0.013. The 11 T species are the cow, sheep, horse and donkey, the dog, red fox and cat, the hedgehog, two moles and a shrew.

Mammals with threonine at GRM1 458 sleep three hours less – and the short sleepers' mutation is also at GRM1 458! Before you write that down, look at the list of T species again, and at stage 5.

3. Species are not independent

A t-test assumes that every data point is independent evidence. Species are not. All 11 T species are laurasiatherians, and all inherited their T from one ancestor, about 79 million years ago (stage 5). They also inherited a great deal else – four of the 11 are hoofed mammals, which sleep little (stage 6) for reasons that may have nothing to do with GRM1. The T and the short sleep could go together simply because both run in the same branch of the tree. In effect there is one change of amino acid behind the 11 species, not 11 (Felsenstein 1985).

A phylogenetic regression allows for this. It expects close relatives to be alike, as much as the tree says they should be, and gives a group of close relatives less weight than the same number of unrelated species. The package phylolm fits it. It needs the tree of the same species, and a table whose row names are the tips' names:

library(ape)
library(phylolm)
tree <- read.tree("/data/examples/fnss/tree/mammals.nwk")
per <- per[per$genome_species %in% tree$tip.label, ]
rownames(per) <- per$genome_species
tr <- keep.tip(tree, per$genome_species)
summary(lm(sleep_pos_h ~ residue, data = per))$coefficients
fit_l <- phylolm(sleep_pos_h ~ residue, data = per, phy = tr, model = "lambda")
summary(fit_l)$coefficients
fit_l$optpar
fit_b <- phylolm(sleep_pos_h ~ residue, data = per, phy = tr, model = "BM")
summary(fit_b)$coefficients

Read the row residueT: its estimate is the difference in hours between T and S species, and the last column its P-value. lm is an ordinary regression – the same test as the t-test above. The "BM" model (Brownian motion) assumes that sleep time drifted along the branches of the tree, so that relatives are as alike as the time since they split predicts; the "lambda" model lets the data say how far that is true: λ = 0 means the tree does not matter, λ = 1 that it matters as much as under Brownian motion (Pagel 1999). fit_l$optpar is the λ that fits best.

Check

Ordinary regression: −3.3 h, P = 0.013 (as the t-test). With the tree, λ = 0.59: −3.1 h, P = 0.09. Under Brownian motion: −2.8 h, P = 0.46. Once the tree is allowed for, the difference is no longer significant.

Think

The estimate barely changed (about −3 hours) – but its uncertainty grew. Why does knowing that the 11 T species are relatives make the evidence weaker, when it does not change the data at all?

5. What the result means

Across mammals, the amino acids at GRM1 458 and SIK3 783 do not go with how long species sleep, once you allow for their family tree. That is a result – but read it carefully:

  • It is not proof of no effect. GRM1 458 changed once: however many species carry T, the test rests on one change, and has little power to detect anything. The sleep data are noisy (stage 6), and only about 50 of the species have both sleep data and sequences.
  • The changes are not the mutations. Mammals have T or S where the short sleepers have A (GRM1) or Y (SIK3). A different amino acid can do something different.
  • What it does show is that these two positions vary freely across mammals, and that nothing in the data links them to sleep. The positions where a change could plausibly shorten sleep across species – DEC2 P384, ADRB1 A187, NPSR1 Y206 – are the ones that never change (stage 4), so the comparison cannot be made for them at all.
