Weeks 8–9about 4 hours

Where are the seven amino acids in the proteins' three-dimensional shapes? In a tightly packed helix, a mutation can change how a protein folds; in a floppy tail, it may change what the protein binds or how it is switched on. AlphaFold's models of the five proteins, and its confidence in each part of them, show which is which.

You will learn
  • what AlphaFold predicts, and how sure it is (pLDDT)
  • the 3D viewer: loading a model, colouring it, showing and labelling residues
  • to read a PDB file in the terminal – and why its columns must be cut by position
  • that the same amino acid can have different numbers in different databases

1. AlphaFold

AlphaFold (Jumper et al. 2021) predicts the 3D structure of a protein from its sequence, and the AlphaFold database holds a model of nearly every known protein. With each amino acid it gives its confidence, the pLDDT, from 0 to 100: above 90 the model is usually as good as an experimental structure; 70–90, the backbone is probably right; below 50, AlphaFold has no idea – usually because the region has no fixed shape at all. Such disordered regions are common, and are often where proteins are switched on and off or bind partners.

2. Read the confidence from the file

The project has the five models as files, in PDB format. Look at one:

cd ~/project
ls /data/examples/fnss/structures
head -4 /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-P08588-F1-model_v6.pdb
grep "^ATOM" /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-P08588-F1-model_v6.pdb | head -3

Each ATOM line is one atom: its number and name (CA is the central carbon of an amino acid, one per residue), the residue's name and chain, the residue's number, the x, y and z coordinates, and two numbers – the second of which, in an AlphaFold model, is the pLDDT. It looks like a table with spaces between the columns, but it is not: each column has a fixed place in the line. When a coordinate is long, it runs into the next one, and the count of "columns" changes:

grep "^ATOM" /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-Q9Y2K2-F1-model_v6.pdb | grep " CA " | awk '{print NF}' | sort | uniq -c
grep "^ATOM" /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-Q9Y2K2-F1-model_v6.pdb | grep " CA " | awk 'NF == 11' | head -2

(NF is awk's number of fields.) So cut the columns by their position in the line instead: characters 18–20 are the residue's name, 23–26 its number, and 61–66 the pLDDT.

grep "^ATOM" /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-P08588-F1-model_v6.pdb | grep " CA " | cut -c 18-20,23-26,61-66 | head -3
grep "^ATOM" /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-P08588-F1-model_v6.pdb | grep " CA " | cut -c 18-20,23-26,61-66 | awk '$2 >= 182 && $2 <= 192'
Check

The SIK3 file has 999 CA lines with 12 fields and 322 with 11: $11 would be the pLDDT on some lines and something else on others. Residues 182–192 of ADRB1 all have pLDDT above 95; A187 has 97.81.

Now the whole of each protein – the number of residues, the mean pLDDT and the share of residues below 50:

for f in /data/examples/fnss/structures/*.pdb
do
  echo "$(basename $f) $(grep '^ATOM' $f | grep ' CA ' | cut -c 61-66 | awk '{n++; s += $1; if ($1 < 50) low++} END {printf "%d residues, mean pLDDT %.1f, %.0f%% below 50", n, s/n, 100 * low / n}')"
done

And the residue of each mutation – the accession and position of each, written by printf one per line, read by a loop:

printf "Q9C0J9\t362\nQ9C0J9\t384\nP08588\t187\nQ6W5P4\t206\nQ13255\t458\n" | while read acc pos
do
  grep "^ATOM" /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-$acc-F1-model_v6.pdb | grep " CA " | cut -c 18-20,23-26,61-66 | awk -v p=$pos -v acc=$acc '$2 == p {print acc, $0}'
done
Check

DEC2 (Q9C0J9): mean pLDDT 55.1, 61% of it below 50; Y362 44.0 and P384 42.4. ADRB1: mean 75.3; A187 97.8. NPSR1 (Q6W5P4): mean 81.4; Y206 93.1. GRM1 (Q13255): mean 70.9; S458 94.8. SIK3 (Q9Y2K2): mean 50.7, 67% below 50.

3. Where is SIK3 N783?

The SIK3 model has 1,321 residues, but your SIK3 protein – the MANE Select one, NP_001353615.1 – has 1,369. UniProt's main SIK3 is another isoform, and its numbers differ. Find N783 in it by its surroundings, as in stage 2: take the 21 amino acids around N783 (from stage 1's mutation_context.tsv), fetch UniProt's sequence with curl, and search:

grep SIK3 results/mutation_context.tsv
curl -s "https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9Y2K2.fasta" > results/SIK3_uniprot.fasta
head -2 results/SIK3_uniprot.fasta
seqtk seq -l0 results/SIK3_uniprot.fasta | tail -1 | grep -o -b "LRIQPSSPPPNHPNNHLFRQP"

The stretch starts 724 letters in, so its first letter is position 725, and N – the eleventh – is position 735. N783 of the MANE protein is N735 of UniProt's (and of the AlphaFold model).

grep "^ATOM" /data/examples/fnss/structures/AF-Q9Y2K2-F1-model_v6.pdb | grep " CA " | cut -c 18-20,23-26,61-66 | awk '$2 == 735'
Check

ASN 735, pLDDT 35.4: N783 is in a disordered region. (If curl cannot reach UniProt, open Q9Y2K2 in the Databases tab and download its FASTA there.)

4. All seven in 3D

Look at each one; for each, write down where it is and how sure AlphaFold is about it.

  • DEC2: a compact, confident part at the start – the bHLH domain that binds DNA, and the Orange domain – then a long, floppy tail. Y362 and P384 are in the tail.
  • NPSR1: seven helices in the membrane, like ADRB1. Y206 is in the loop between the fourth and fifth helices, outside the cell (extracellular loop 2) – a loop that, in many receptors for peptides, forms part of the lid of the binding pocket. N107, the common variant, is in the first extracellular loop.
  • GRM1: a large two-lobed domain outside the cell that closes on glutamate like a Venus flytrap, a domain rich in cysteines, the seven helices, and the long tail of mGluR1a. S458 is in the flytrap, confidently placed.
  • SIK3: a kinase domain at the start, then most of the protein disordered – including N783.
  • R889W (mGluR1b) has no model: AlphaFold models only UniProt's main isoform, mGluR1a, whose tail at 889 has another sequence (and pLDDT 36).
Think

Three of the seven mutations (P384R, Y362H, N783Y) are in disordered regions, three (A187V, Y206H, S458A) in confidently folded ones. Does that match what you found in stage 4 – are the folded ones the conserved ones? (Careful: S458 is folded but not conserved.) What could a mutation in a disordered tail do?
