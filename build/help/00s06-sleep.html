Stage 6 · How long mammals sleep

Weeks 6–7about 4 hours

Before you ask whether the amino acids go with sleep, get to know the sleep data: where they come from, how much they vary, how far two compilations agree, and what else goes with sleeping little or a lot.

You will learn
  • to explore a table in R: summary, sorting, picking rows and columns
  • histograms, scatter plots and box plots
  • correlation, and how to test it
  • where numbers in a published table come from – and how much to trust them

1. The sleep table

Open the notebook of this stage and read the table:

sleep <- read.delim("/data/examples/fnss/sleep/mammal_sleep.tsv")
dim(sleep)
names(sleep)
head(sleep, 3)

One row per species. Two columns hold the hours of sleep in 24 hours, from two compilations of published studies (The project data has the details):

  • sleep_pos_h – the Phylogeny of Sleep database (McNamara et al. 2008), using only recordings that meet the criteria of Capellini et al. (2008): sleep measured with the EEG, for at least 12 hours, in adult animals on land; the median of a species' recordings.
  • sleep_msleep_h – the msleep table of Savage & West (2007), with body mass.

Where a species has no value, R shows NA ("not available"); summary counts them:

summary(sleep$sleep_pos_h)
summary(sleep$sleep_msleep_h)
sum(!is.na(sleep$sleep_pos_h))
sum(!is.na(sleep$sleep_msleep_h))
Check

101 species and 10 columns. The Phylogeny of Sleep has values for 66 species (3.1 to 20.4 hours, median 10.5), msleep for 77 (1.9 to 19.9, median 10.3).

2. Who sleeps least, and who most?

order gives the order of the rows that sorts a column; use it to pick the rows, and choose the columns by name:

by_sleep <- sleep[order(sleep$sleep_pos_h), c("common_name", "order", "sleep_pos_h")]
head(by_sleep, 5)
by_sleep[!is.na(by_sleep$sleep_pos_h), ][62:66, ]
sleep[which.min(sleep$sleep_msleep_h), c("common_name", "sleep_msleep_h")]
sleep[which.max(sleep$sleep_msleep_h), c("common_name", "sleep_msleep_h")]
sleep[sleep$species == "Homo sapiens", c("common_name", "sleep_pos_h", "sleep_msleep_h")]
hist(sleep$sleep_pos_h, breaks = seq(0, 22, 1), col = "grey80",
     main = "Daily sleep of 66 mammal species", xlab = "Hours of sleep in 24 hours")
Check

Least (Phylogeny of Sleep): the donkey (3.1 h), cow, horse, sheep and the yellow-bellied marmot (3.9 h). Most: three armadillos and two opossums, up to the large hairy armadillo (20.4 h). In msleep the least is the giraffe (1.9 h), the most the little brown bat (19.9 h). Humans: 8 hours in msleep, none in the Phylogeny of Sleep table.

3. Do the two compilations agree?

42 species are in both. A scatter plot with the line on which the two would be equal (abline(0, 1): intercept 0, slope 1), and their correlation, r: 1 if one rises exactly in step with the other, 0 if they have nothing to do with each other:

both <- sleep[!is.na(sleep$sleep_pos_h) & !is.na(sleep$sleep_msleep_h), ]
nrow(both)
plot(both$sleep_msleep_h, both$sleep_pos_h, pch = 19, xlab = "msleep (hours)", ylab = "Phylogeny of Sleep (hours)")
abline(0, 1, lty = 2)
cor.test(both$sleep_pos_h, both$sleep_msleep_h)
both$diff <- both$sleep_pos_h - both$sleep_msleep_h
both[abs(both$diff) > 3, c("common_name", "sleep_pos_h", "sleep_msleep_h")]
Check

r = 0.84 (P = 3 × 10−12): the two agree well, but not perfectly. Five species differ by more than 3 hours: the three-toed sloth (8.4 vs 14.4), the golden-mantled ground squirrel (8.7 vs 15.9), the European hedgehog, the short-nosed echidna and the tree shrew.

Think

Why might two tables give a species such different values? The compilations partly use the same studies, and partly different ones: animals recorded for different lengths of time, at different ages and seasons, in a cage with food and no predators or in the wild, with or without the EEG. What does this mean for any analysis that uses one number per species?

4. What goes with sleep?

By order – a box plot, one box per order: the thick line is the median, the box holds the middle half of the species. par(mar = …) makes room under the plot for the names, and las = 2 turns them sideways:

par(mar = c(9, 4, 2, 1))
boxplot(sleep_pos_h ~ order, data = sleep, las = 2, xlab = "", ylab = "Hours of sleep (Phylogeny of Sleep)")
round(sort(tapply(sleep$sleep_pos_h, sleep$order, median, na.rm = TRUE)), 1)

And by size. Body masses go from a few grams to tonnes, so plot them on a log scale (log10: 1 is 10 kg, 2 is 100 kg, −2 is 10 g):

par(mar = c(5, 4, 2, 1))
plot(log10(sleep$body_mass_kg), sleep$sleep_msleep_h, pch = 19,
     xlab = "Body mass (log10 kg)", ylab = "Hours of sleep (msleep)")
cor.test(log10(sleep$body_mass_kg), sleep$sleep_msleep_h)
Check

The hoofed mammals sleep least (median 3.4 h for horses and their relatives, 3.5 h for the even-toed ones), opossums and armadillos most (18–19 h); primates and carnivores sleep about 10 h, rodents 12. The bigger the mammal, the less it sleeps: r = −0.55 over 77 species (P = 3 × 10−7).

5. Sleep in the sea

table(sleep$environment)
sleep[sleep$environment == "marine", c("common_name", "sleep_pos_h", "sleep_msleep_h")]

Five marine mammals – dolphin, porpoise, three seals – have values only in msleep. Dolphins and porpoises sleep with one half of the brain at a time, and seals sleep differently in water and on land; the criteria of the Phylogeny of Sleep table leave marine records out, because their sleep cannot be compared with that of land mammals in the same way. Keep this in mind in stage 7: the whales and dolphins are 45 of the 108 species with S at SIK3 783 – and none of them has a value in the Phylogeny of Sleep table.

6. Which species have sequences?

The sleep studies were done on the species that were at hand; the genomes were sequenced for other reasons. The last two columns link them: genome_species is the species of the sequence files that stands for each sleep species – the species itself, or one of the same genus – and match says which:

table(sleep$match)
sleep[sleep$match == "genus", c("species", "genome_species")]
Check

64 species have sequences of their own, 16 a species of the same genus, and 21 none.
